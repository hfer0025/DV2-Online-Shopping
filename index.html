Add to Cart: How the World Shops Online

Who buys online, when, what they buy, and where it comes from.

9.8M households · +14% on 2024 · 1 in 3 dollars from NSW

Part 01

The Global Cart

Online shopping is near-universal in some countries and rare in others. In 2021, 77% of Australian adults bought something online, close to the Nordic leaders, while in much of South Asia and Africa fewer than 1 in 10 did. The gap mostly follows income and internet access, but it is closing fast in emerging markets.

Part 02

Australia Logs On

A decade of steady growth, then COVID lockdowns pushed online sales into a new normal. Annual online retail sales rose from $20.8B in 2019 to $49.9B in 2024. Shoppers who were forced online in 2020 and 2021 mostly stayed, and every year the end-of-year sales season sets new records.

Part 03

Where in Australia

The big eastern states spend the most in total: NSW alone spent $26.4B, about one in every three online dollars. But totals mostly reflect population. Divide by the number of people and the picture changes: Canberrans are the most active online shoppers, while the Northern Territory trails well behind.

Part 04

What We Buy and Who Buys It

Marketplaces like Amazon, eBay and Temu take the biggest slice of online spending, ahead of groceries and fashion. Every category grew in 2025, with department stores up almost 20%. And Millennials spend more than a third of every online dollar, though the oldest shoppers are growing fastest.

Part 05

Beyond Our Borders

43% of Australian online shoppers buy from overseas, mostly from the USA and China. Lower prices and a wider range pull them in; slow delivery and wanting to support local businesses hold them back.

What It All Means

01

Online shopping is now normal for most Australians. 82% of households shopped online in 2025.

02

COVID was the turning point. Online's share of retail doubled and never went back.

03

Where you live matters. Per person, Canberrans spend almost twice as much as Territorians.

04

Shopping is global. 43% buy from overseas, mostly to save money, despite slower delivery.
